# Chapter 6: Recommendations and Limitations of the Study

## 6.1 Recommendations

Informed by critical review of literature, verified secondary data, comparative case analysis, and findings consolidated in Chapter 5, the following recommendations are proposed for banks, operations leaders, and consultancy advisors including Adhiita Consultancy Services, Noida.

1. **Establish an operations-led AI and automation steering committee** comprising representatives from operations, IT, risk, compliance, and business lines. Technology initiatives should be sanctioned against operational KPIs—not IT budgets alone—to align investment with processing efficiency, quality, and customer outcomes.

2. **Conduct enterprise-wide process mining and automation opportunity assessment** prior to tool procurement. Document as-is workflows, quantify volumes and exception rates, and rank processes through an impact-versus-feasibility matrix. Refrain from automating defective processes; redesign first where defect rates or handoffs are excessive.

3. **Invest in data foundation concurrently with automation pilots.** Establish unified customer and transaction identifiers, improve metadata quality, and deploy feature stores or data lakes accessible to authorised analytics teams. AI models and bots fail silently when underlying data is incomplete or inconsistent.

4. **Adopt a phased implementation roadmap:** assess, prioritize, pilot, scale, optimize. Each phase requires defined exit criteria, budget gates, and governance checkpoints, reducing the risk of enterprise rollout before stable operation is demonstrated.

5. **Scale RPA into intelligent automation** by integrating bots with machine learning for exception classification, NLP for document extraction, and API-based integration rather than screen-scraping alone. Screen-scraping bots are fragile when user interfaces change.

6. **Implement human-in-the-loop controls for high-impact decisions** including credit denial, fraud account freezing, and AML escalation. Fully autonomous decisions in these domains increase conduct and legal risk unless models are interpretable, validated, and regularly audited.

7. **Develop AI governance policies aligned with emerging regulatory expectations**, including model inventories, validation documentation, bias testing for credit and customer treatment models, and incident response procedures for model failure or data breach.

8. **Launch structured workforce upskilling programs** repositioning operations staff from repetitive processing to exception management, quality assurance of automated outputs, and customer empathy roles. Communicate automation as augmentation where accurate; address displacement concerns transparently.

9. **Standardize vendor evaluation criteria** covering interoperability with core banking systems, data residency, explainability features, exit clauses, service level agreements, and supervisory audit access. Reduce vendor lock-in through modular architecture and open APIs where feasible.

10. **Expand customer service automation with seamless escalation design.** Chatbots should identify intent accurately, disclose non-human interaction where required, and transfer context to human agents without forcing customers to repeat information—closing the gap between back-office efficiency and front-end experience scores.

11. **Prioritize reconciliation and back-office modernization** after initial fraud and KYC automation wins. These domains lag in adoption but accumulate operational cost and error risk; API-enabled core banking migration or wrapper layers should be planned with realistic multi-year timelines.

12. **Deploy operations control tower dashboards** integrating real-time metrics on queue depth, SLA breaches, bot failure rates, fraud alert backlog, and compliance deadlines. Trigger automated remedial workflows or leadership alerts when thresholds are breached.

13. **Pilot generative AI internally before customer deployment** for use cases such as procedure summarization, test case generation, and agent assist. Prohibit upload of confidential customer data to public models; use enterprise-hosted or bank-approved environments with logging and review.

14. **Benchmark maturity annually** using a balanced scorecard: automation coverage, cycle time reduction, error rates, cost per transaction, model validation status, employee training hours, and customer effort scores. Benchmarking against peer institutions identifies gaps and justifies investment.

15. **Partner with consultancy and academic institutions** for independent program reviews, change management support, and talent pipelines. External perspective helps overcome internal inertia and validates business cases for board approval.

## 6.2 Limitations of the Study

1. **Secondary-data-only design:** No primary survey or interviews were conducted; all statistics derive from published reports which may reflect reporting bias.

2. **Incomplete bank disclosure comparability:** Not all five banks publish numeric digital operations metrics suitable for cross-bank ranking; this study reports only publicly disclosed figures.

3. **Source currency:** Industry surveys span 2022–2025; rapid GenAI evolution may date some figures quickly.

4. **Geographic mix:** Deloitte banking analytics survey uses US executives; EY data is India-focused—direct comparison requires caution.

5. **Public disclosure limitation:** Banks disclose positive digital metrics selectively; internal failure rates unavailable.

6. **No proprietary KPI access:** Cannot verify claimed 30-minute loan approval or 71% digital trade against internal audit data.

7. **Case sample size:** Five banks only; smaller regional and cooperative banks not represented.

8. **English-language source bias:** RBI and Big Four reports in English; regional language operations not captured.

9. **Projection vs. verified:** EY 2030 productivity projections (46%) are forecasts, not realized outcomes.

10. **Academic scope:** MBA timeline limits primary data collection; secondary design is appropriate but not equivalent to funded research study.

Notwithstanding these constraints, the triangulated methodology offers credible directional guidance for operations management strategy in banking AI and automation adoption.
